How main screens are picked

Monday Night Football is the only sound game on Mondays. The one exception: a local team playing a championship or postseason game (for example the Braves in the NLCS) also gets sound.
Championship / postseason next. On every other day, any playoff or title game (World Series, NBA Finals, Stanley Cup, March Madness, etc.) is a main screen.
Football owns football days. Whenever NFL or college football is on (Thursday through Monday in season), NBA, NHL and college basketball run on the side screens only. On days with no football they compete for sound like any other game, local teams first.
Local teams next. Each store has a weighted list of its home college and pro teams across football, basketball, hockey and baseball. The Atlanta stores lean Georgia, Georgia Tech, Falcons, Hawks and Braves; the Carolinas lean Clemson, South Carolina, UNC, NC State, Duke, Panthers, Hornets and Hurricanes; Huntsville leans Alabama and Auburn; Smyrna leans Tennessee, Vanderbilt, Titans and Predators.
Then the size of the game. AP Top 25 rankings, ranked-vs-ranked matchups, rivalries and national primetime windows push a game up.
One sound game at a time. The highest-priority game owns its window. Nothing else gets sound while it is on, and a smaller game only takes over once it ends. A gold Sound from / Sound until line on a card tells the floor exactly when to switch. Every other game runs on the side screens.
Sources: NFL schedule via CBS Sports · college football kickoffs and TV via FBSchedules.com · AP Top 25 (Nov 8) via CBS Sports · MLB postseason via MLB.com. Channel lineups from the Stars and Strikes programming guide (DirecTV).
